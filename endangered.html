The Animals We Could Lose

Philippine wildlife is rich, unique, and vulnerable. Learn about selected threatened animals and the habitats they depend on.

Wildlife in the Philippines

Saving a species starts with protecting its home.

Wildlife protection is not a photo opportunity.

This highlight represents the careful work of people who help wildlife while following proper conservation and rehabilitation practices.

Endangered animals are not simply a collection of rare photographs. Their survival depends on forests, wetlands, islands, food sources, breeding areas, and the people and institutions protecting those places.

The Philippine examples below are presented as a learning list rather than individual pages. The information is based on the data and research gathered for this project and is intended to help readers understand the animals, their habitats, and the threats they face. For careful verification and deeper reading, consult the current sources listed below.

Several threats appear repeatedly: habitat loss and degradation, hunting, illegal wildlife collection or trade, and human disturbance. Protecting a species therefore means addressing the causes of decline as well as caring for individual animals.

Research & reliable sources

The information in this feature is based on the data and research gathered for this project. To verify conservation status, wildlife classifications, and current assessments carefully, readers are encouraged to explore reliable primary and specialist sources.

  • Department of Environment and Natural Resources (DENR) — Philippine environmental and wildlife information.
  • DENR Biodiversity Management Bureau (BMB) — biodiversity and threatened-wildlife programs, lists, and conservation information.
  • IUCN Red List of Threatened Species — international species assessments and conservation-status information.
Critically Endangered

Philippine Eagle

Habitat: Large forests in Luzon, Samar, Leyte, and Mindanao.

Threats: Deforestation, habitat loss, and hunting.

Population: About 400 pairs remain in the wild, according to the supplied research.

Critically Endangered

Tamaraw

Habitat: Grasslands and forests of Mindoro, especially Mt. Iglit-Baco Natural Park.

Threats: Habitat degradation, hunting, and human activities.

Population: The supplied research reports 425 individuals recorded in Mt. Iglit-Baco Natural Park in 2026.

Critically Endangered

Philippine Crocodile

Habitat: Freshwater rivers, lakes, and wetlands.

Threats: Habitat destruction, hunting, and human disturbance.

Population: Only a small number remain in the wild; an exact nationwide population is difficult to determine.

Critically Endangered

Visayan Spotted Deer

Habitat: Remaining forests and grasslands of the Visayan Islands.

Threats: Deforestation, hunting, and agricultural expansion.

Population: No dependable current total; small, fragmented populations remain.

Critically Endangered

Visayan Warty Pig

Habitat: Forests and grasslands of the Visayan Islands.

Threats: Habitat loss, hunting, and conversion of forests to agricultural land.

Population: Exact population is unknown; wild populations are small and fragmented.

Critically Endangered

Sulu Hornbill

Habitat: Remaining forests of islands in the Sulu Archipelago.

Threats: Forest loss and degradation.

Population: Fewer than 50 individuals are estimated to remain in the supplied research.

Critically Endangered

Philippine Cockatoo

Habitat: Lowland forests, mangroves, and coastal areas, especially in Palawan.

Threats: Habitat destruction and illegal capture for the pet trade.

Population: The supplied research describes the wild population as very small and declining.

Endangered

Palawan Pangolin

Habitat: Forests and other wooded areas of Palawan.

Threats: Illegal hunting, wildlife trafficking, and habitat loss.

Population: No reliable total wild population estimate is available in the supplied research; it is considered declining.

Endangered

Philippine Tarsier

Habitat: Forests, secondary forests, and shrublands in Bohol, Samar, Leyte, and Mindanao.

Threats: Habitat loss, deforestation, and human disturbance.

Population: Exact population is unknown; the supplied research notes decline linked to habitat loss.

Critically Endangered

Cebu Flowerpecker

Habitat: Forests and remaining forest patches in Cebu.

Threats: Deforestation and habitat loss.

Population: Very small and fragmented population.
