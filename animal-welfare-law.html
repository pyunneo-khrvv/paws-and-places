When Kindness Becomes a Legal Duty

Philippine animal welfare law sets standards for humane treatment, responsible care, and the protection of animals.

Know the law

RA 8485 and RA 10631

Knowing the law makes responsible action easier.

This member highlight represents the legal side of animal welfare and the importance of knowing the rules that protect animals.

Republic Act No. 8485, known as the Animal Welfare Act of 1998, was enacted to promote and protect the welfare of animals in the Philippines. Republic Act No. 10631, approved in 2013, amended important provisions of that law and strengthened animal protection.

RA 10631 defines animal welfare in terms of the physical and psychological well-being of animals. It includes appropriate accommodation, feeding and general care, prevention and treatment of disease, freedom from fear and distress, and the ability to express normal behavior.

The law prohibits acts such as torture, maltreatment, cruelty, and neglect. It also makes abandonment of an animal under a person's custody unlawful when the animal is left without care. The law addresses animal fighting and other forms of abuse as well.

Another important point is wildlife habitat. The original Animal Welfare Act states that it is the duty of every person to protect the natural habitat of wildlife and recognizes destruction of that habitat as a form of cruelty.

RA 10631 also provides graduated penalties for cruelty, maltreatment, or neglect depending on the seriousness and outcome of the violation. Because legal cases can depend on the facts and applicable laws, suspected violations should be reported to the proper authorities rather than handled through personal retaliation.

Important: This page is an educational summary, not legal advice. For a real incident, consult the complete law and the appropriate government authority.

Primary legal sources

  • Republic Act No. 8485 — Animal Welfare Act of 1998.
  • Republic Act No. 10631 — amendments to RA 8485, including the definition of animal welfare, abandonment, and graduated penalties.
