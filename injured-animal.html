What If You Find an Animal in Trouble?

A calm response can prevent a bad situation from becoming worse. Safety comes first—for you and the animal.

Emergency response

Help without becoming the second emergency.

A rescuer's first job is to assess, not rush.

This highlight represents safe rescue behavior: keeping distance, gathering useful information, and contacting the right professionals.

If you find an injured animal, protect yourself first. A frightened, painful, or wild animal can bite, scratch, kick, or otherwise injure a person even when it normally seems harmless.

1

Make the area safer.

Keep children and pets away. Do not approach an aggressive, frightened, or wild animal. If you are near traffic, avoid putting yourself in danger.

2

Do not force treatment.

Do not force-feed food or water and do not give human medicine. Avoid unnecessary handling, especially with wildlife.

3

Identify the kind of animal.

For a pet or stray cat/dog, contact a veterinary clinic or animal rescue organization. For wildlife, contact DENR/PENRO or the appropriate wildlife authority instead of taking the animal home.

4

Record useful information.

From a safe distance, take a photo if possible and note the location. This can help responders understand what animal they are dealing with and where it was found.

5

If a person is bitten or scratched, get medical care.

Human health matters too. Seek prompt medical attention after an animal bite or scratch, particularly when the animal's vaccination or disease status is unknown.

🐍 Snake at home

Stay calm, keep your distance, keep children and pets away, and do not try to catch, touch, or kill it. Contact appropriate local authorities or DENR wildlife personnel.

🐣 Baby wildlife

Do not immediately assume a young wild animal is abandoned. Parents may be nearby. Observe from a distance and contact wildlife authorities if it is injured or clearly needs assistance.

🐒 Wildlife on a road

Do not chase or corner it. Keep people away, avoid handling, and contact wildlife authorities so trained personnel can decide what should happen next.

Philippine wildlife response

DENR CALABARZON reports that injured, orphaned, or turned-over wildlife can be handled through its wildlife rescue program, including the Regional Wildlife Rescue Center in Calauan, Laguna.
