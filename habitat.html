A Wild Animal Needs a Wild Home

Wildlife protection is not complete when an animal is rescued. The habitat it depends on matters just as much.

Beyond the animal

Protecting wildlife means protecting places.

A closer look at why protecting natural habitats matters to wildlife.

Forests, wetlands, grasslands, rivers, mangroves, and coastal areas are not just scenery. They provide food, shelter, breeding sites, and the conditions wildlife needs to survive.

For example, the Philippine eagle depends on large forests. The tamaraw is associated with grasslands and forests of Mindoro. The Philippine crocodile depends on freshwater environments, while the Philippine cockatoo uses lowland forests, mangroves, and coastal areas, particularly in Palawan. The Philippine tarsier is associated with forests, secondary forests, and shrublands.

When habitats are destroyed or fragmented, animals may lose food sources, nesting or breeding areas, shelter, and safe routes between places they need. This is why wildlife conservation cannot focus only on individual animals.

Forests

Important for species such as the Philippine eagle, tarsier, hornbills, and many other forest-dependent animals.

Freshwater

Rivers, lakes, wetlands, and other freshwater habitats are essential to species such as the Philippine crocodile.

Coasts & islands

Mangroves, coastal areas, and island forests support wildlife such as the Philippine cockatoo and many other species.

Sometimes the best way to save an animal is not to take it somewhere else—it is to keep its natural home safe.

What the law says

The Animal Welfare Act states that it is the duty of every person to protect the natural habitat of wildlife and recognizes destruction of wildlife habitat as a form of cruelty. Wildlife protection is also addressed by other Philippine environmental and wildlife laws.
